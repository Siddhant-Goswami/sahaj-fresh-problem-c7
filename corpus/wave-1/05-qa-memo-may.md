**Subject:** Category A returns at Tier 2 hubs: root cause and corrective action

---

## 1. Summary

Category A returns at NSK-1 and IND-1 have risen from a baseline of 0.3 per cent of despatched units to 2.1 per cent over the six weeks to 17 May. The complaint text is consistent: curd described as watery, loose or sour at the point of delivery. Tier 1 hubs are flat over the same window.

The **root cause** is the reduced pouch film gauge introduced with the change of flexible packaging supplier in February. Under Tier 2 ambient conditions the thinner film does not hold seal integrity through the delivery window. Corrective action at section 5.

## 2. What changed

Effective 12 February 2024 the company moved its flexible packaging for Category A pouches from M/s Sanchit Polyfilms to M/s Nivaan Flexipack Private Limited under the procurement rationalisation, on a single national contract covering all hub supply with delivery into the regional converters at Hosur and Pithampur. The new film is a three-layer laminate at **55 micron** against the previous **65 micron**.

The specification was accepted on the basis of Nivaan's declared burst strength, which is marginally better than Sanchit's despite the lower gauge.

## 3. Testing performed

QA lab, Bengaluru, 6 to 16 May. Samples drawn from the Nivaan lot NVF/24/0409 and from retained Sanchit stock.

| Test | Method | Sanchit 65 mic | Nivaan 55 mic | Spec |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seal peel strength, ambient 25 C | Internal QA-M-12 | 21.4 N/15mm | 19.8 N/15mm | >= 18.0 |
| Seal peel strength, conditioned 40 C for 4 hr | Internal QA-M-12 | 18.9 N/15mm | **14.2 N/15mm** | >= 18.0 |
| Drop test, 1.2 m, filled | Internal QA-M-08 | 0/20 fail | 3/20 fail | <= 1/20 |
| Drop test after 40 C conditioning | Internal QA-M-08 | 1/20 fail | **6/20 fail** | <= 1/20 |
| Burst strength, ambient | Supplier COA | 218 kPa | 226 kPa | >= 200 |

The two conditioned results are the finding. At ambient the Nivaan film is inside specification. After four hours at 40 degrees C it is not, on two of five measures.

## 4. Why only the Tier 2 hubs

This is the part that reconciles the geography. Bengaluru does not see sustained 40 degree ambient in April and May; daytime highs in the delivery window sit in the high twenties. Nashik and Indore both do. The film is therefore inside specification for the climate it was validated in and outside specification for the climate the Tier 2 hubs actually operate in.

The pattern is exactly what a thermally driven seal failure looks like: it appears in the hot cities, it appears in the early summer, it appears on the product with the highest moisture content (curd) and not on paneer or milk pouches, which carry a different seal geometry.

Support's reading in April that this is "the season" was half right. The season is the trigger. The film is the cause.

## 5. Corrective action proposed

5.1 Revert Category A pouch film to 65 micron for NSK-1 and IND-1 supply with immediate effect. Nivaan have confirmed they can produce at 65 micron at a cost of Rs 0.34 per pouch over the current rate.

5.2 Retain 55 micron at BLR-1, BLR-2 and PNQ-1 where the validated climate envelope holds. This preserves roughly 70 per cent of the packaging saving.

5.3 Add a 40 degree C conditioned seal test to the inbound acceptance protocol for all flexible packaging. This gap in QA-M-12 is ours and I am owning it.

5.4 Re-baseline the Tier 2 return rate at four weeks post-changeover. I expect a return to under 0.5 per cent.

## 6. Not investigated further

Cold room temperatures at both Tier 2 hubs were pulled for the period and are within band. Rider handling was reviewed with the hub managers in April and a crate staging briefing was issued at NSK-1 on 27 April; returns continued after it, which is further evidence that handling is not the driver.

I consider this closed pending the film changeover.
